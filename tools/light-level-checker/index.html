Window Light Level Checker

Answer a few observations about one plant location to get a practical low, medium, or strong light estimate.

Describe the spot

This is a simple observation-based guide, not a light meter or a species-specific growing recommendation. Sun angles, latitude, season, glazing, and nearby shade change the result.

How to use the result

Check the spot on a clear day and note when direct sunlight actually lands on the leaves. A south-facing window in much of the United States often receives more direct sun than a north-facing window, but buildings, trees, season, and latitude can change that pattern. The checker combines your answers as a rough comparison, not a measured scientific category.

Compare two possible locations by checking each one separately. Recheck when seasons change or when a tree leafs out. A plant’s growth can also help you decide whether its current position is suitable; the tool does not diagnose a plant or promise that a spot will work for every species.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a south-facing window always mean strong light?

No. Nearby buildings, overhangs, trees, window glass, latitude, and season affect how much direct light reaches a plant.

What does “medium light” mean in this checker?

It is a practical estimate for a spot with useful daylight but without the strongest combination of direct sun, close distance, and an unobstructed view.

Can this checker tell me which plant will thrive?

No. Compare the result with the light needs of the exact species and observe the plant after placement. This tool does not measure light intensity.

When should I check the spot again?

Repeat the observation when seasons change, nearby trees leaf out, furniture moves, or the plant is moved to another location.
