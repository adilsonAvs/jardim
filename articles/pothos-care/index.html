Pothos Care: Light, Watering, and Pruning

By Adilson V. Santos · Published October 5, 2026 · Updated November 22, 2026 · 5 min read

Pothos tolerates a range of indoor conditions, yet its growth still reflects the light and watering it receives. Use leaf and stem changes to adjust care.

Quick Take

Give pothos bright, indirect light when possible, let the mix partly dry, and prune with clean tools to manage length.

Training a pothos vine

A pothos can trail from a shelf or climb a support, but the support should be stable and the stems should not be tied tightly. Guide one vine at a time and check that leaves are not pressed against a cold window or wall. If you want a fuller pot, rooted cuttings from the same plant may be placed back into the container when established. Use clean tools and retain a node on a cutting. Pothos sap can irritate some people, so wash hands after pruning and keep cuttings away from pets and children until you have verified safety guidance for your household.

Prune to control length or encourage branching

Pruning changes the shape or length of the existing plant. Choose the stem you want to shorten and cut just above a node with clean scissors; a node is where a leaf joins the stem. Avoid removing a large share of healthy foliage at once. If light is causing long gaps between leaves, improve the location gradually as well—repeated cutting alone will not correct the cause. For general pruning technique, see how to prune houseplants safely.

Take a cutting when the goal is a new plant

Propagation uses a piece of stem to grow another plant; a leaf blade without a node will not produce a new vine. With clean scissors, take a modest cutting that includes a node and at least one healthy leaf, while leaving plenty of foliage on the parent. Place the node in water or a suitable moist propagation medium and keep leaves above the surface. Give it bright indirect light and warmth, then check for root development rather than relying on a fixed number of days. Once roots are established, move it gently to a small draining pot and keep the mix lightly moist without leaving it saturated. For a fuller pot, return established cuttings only when there is room and drainage. The dedicated plant propagation guide explains the broader process.

Use new growth to evaluate a care change

Compare the newest leaves and stem spacing before adjusting care. Long gaps between leaves, smaller foliage, or a strong lean toward a window can suggest the plant needs more usable light; bleached or scorched patches can follow harsh direct sun. These signs are clues rather than a diagnosis. Note the current position and change one condition at a time, then wait for new growth before deciding whether the adjustment helped. If leaves yellow while the mix stays wet, check drainage before adding water or fertilizer. For fine stippling or webbing, use the identification steps in the spider mite guide. Pothos sap can irritate some people; wash hands after handling it and verify pet-safety guidance for the exact plant.

Choose a spot by the shape of new growth

Pothos is adaptable, but “low light” does not mean no daylight. In a north-facing room, start near the brightest unobstructed window and compare the newest leaves after several weeks. If stems lengthen with wide gaps between leaves, variegation fades, or growth slows markedly, move the pot closer to indirect light. A sheer curtain can soften strong afternoon sun. Keep leaves from pressing against cold glass in winter, and rotate the pot a quarter turn occasionally if growth leans strongly toward the window.

Light changes how quickly the mix dries. A small pot near a bright window may need moisture checks much more often than a larger pot farther back. Do not set a weekly watering rule; Virginia Cooperative Extension advises frequent moisture monitoring because plant, container, and season affect drying. Push a finger into the top couple of inches, lift the pot to learn its dry weight, and water thoroughly when the upper mix has dried to the depth appropriate for the pot. Let excess drain. In a cool, dim room, the same volume of water can remain in the mix longer, so inspect before repeating.

Pruning and propagation are different cuts

For a shorter plant, cut a vine just above a node—the point where a leaf and aerial root emerge. Several cuts can encourage growth from nodes farther back, but they will not make a bare stem leafy if those nodes are dead. For propagation, each cutting needs at least one viable node; a leaf alone may stay green for a while without producing a new vine. Place the node in clean water or a suitable moist propagation medium, keep the leaf above the surface, and provide bright indirect light. Change stagnant water and pot rooted cuttings only after roots are established enough to handle the transition.

To make a fuller display, plant several rooted cuttings back into the same pot only if the container has room and drains well. Avoid packing too many stems into a small nursery pot: crowded roots and persistently wet mix invite decline. Pothos contains insoluble calcium oxalate crystals and is listed as toxic to cats and dogs by the ASPCA. Hang or place vines where pets cannot chew them; trailing stems can make access easier rather than safer.

Read common symptoms before changing several things

Long bare sections: often indicate insufficient light or pruning history. Improve the light gradually and trim selectively above healthy nodes.

Yellow leaves with wet, heavy mix: reduce watering frequency and check that drainage holes are open; do not respond with fertilizer.

Brown, crispy edges: check whether the root ball became very dry, whether direct sun is scorching leaves, and whether salts have accumulated. Change one factor at a time.

Drooping while mix is damp: investigate root health and drainage rather than watering again. A pot without drainage needs especially careful management and is usually better used as a cachepot around a draining nursery pot.

Wipe dust from broad leaves with a damp soft cloth and inspect their undersides while doing so. If fine stippling or webbing appears, isolate the plant and use the inspection steps in How to Identify Spider Mites on Houseplants. For safe trimming, see How to Prune Houseplants Safely; cuttings are covered in How to Propagate Plants from Cuttings.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should I water pothos?

Check the potting mix rather than watering on a fixed weekday. Let it dry moderately between waterings, then consider the pot size, light, and season.

Where should I cut a pothos vine?

For pruning, cut just above a node. A propagation cutting needs a node because that is where new growth can form.

Can pothos grow in low light?

It tolerates lower indoor light, but leaf color and growth can suffer. Compare new leaves and stem spacing before moving it closer to brighter indirect light.

Are pothos leaves toxic to cats and dogs?

Yes. ASPCA lists pothos, also called devil’s ivy, as toxic to cats and dogs. Keep vines inaccessible and contact a veterinarian if a pet chews the plant.

Can I make a pothos fuller by cutting it?

Selective pruning above healthy nodes can encourage branching, but light and root health also matter. Root viable node cuttings and return them to the pot only if space and drainage allow.
